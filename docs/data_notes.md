# ScentLens data notes

## Source

The catalog is the Hugging Face dataset doevent/perfume, pinned to revision 9d2db452d8f0d649c4bfb0189a2fcc5282028a3c. The dataset card describes it as a "RAW Perfume dataset (2024 Year), over 26k perfumes. Images, ingredients, description, etc.", created 01.05.2024, with Max Skobeev listed as the card author. The card carries the license tag mit and the tag not-for-all-audiences.

The card does not say where the perfume images or descriptions were collected from. The rights to the underlying images are not verified. The MIT tag on the card is the uploader's statement about the dataset; it does not establish that the uploader held the rights to the product photos. ScentLens therefore never publishes catalog images: they appear nowhere in this repository, its charts, or the demo. Only names and text metadata are shown, and the demo Space holds image embeddings and metadata derived from the dataset, not the images.

Files used: perfumes.csv (metadata) and images.zip (product photos). Images are read straight from the archive without extracting it.

Flagged images were never displayed or saved; only their counts are logged. The images dropped as not a bottle photo were mostly cream jars or lotion tubes (33) and blank images (21).

Spot check: 50 kept images drawn at random (pandas sample, random_state 7) were viewed locally. All 50 were single bottle product photos on a white background; none was inappropriate, a person, or a non bottle item. The temporary contact sheets were deleted after viewing.

## Correctness rules

* Strict: the predicted product is the same product group as the truth. A product group is all catalog rows with the same normalized brand and normalized name. Normalization lowercases, strips accents and punctuation, removes concentration words (eau de toilette, eau de parfum, eau de cologne, extrait de parfum, edt, edp, edc, parfum, perfume, cologne, intense, extrait) and four digit years, and drops a repeated brand prefix from the name.
* Lenient: same normalized brand and the same line key, where the line key is the normalized name with flanker words removed (noir, sport, extreme, absolu, elixir, fraiche, night, nuit, summer, edition, limited, collector, platinum, black, rouge, blanc, pure, essence, light, and articles). So a Sauvage Elixir prediction for a Sauvage bottle is lenient correct but strict wrong.
* A bottle that is not in the catalog has no correct answer: any answer is incorrect, and only abstaining is right.

Both rules and the normalization are unit tested in tests/test_normalize.py and tests/test_matching.py.

## Known label noise

* Packaging changes. A product's bottle or label may have changed across release years and reformulations. The catalog holds one photo per listing, often of one specific edition, so a real bottle from a different year can look different from its reference. Some strict mismatches are therefore catalog noise, not model errors. This is not measured.
* Near identical bottles. Many brands reuse one bottle shape across a line (flankers, colorways, concentrations). Their reference photos can be near identical, so strict identity is sometimes not recoverable from the image alone. The lenient rule partly absorbs this.
* Generic brand images. Some brands use one picture for many products; those images were dropped (see Cleaning).
* Names. Brand and name spellings vary across listings (accents, abbreviations, repeated brand names). Normalization merges the common cases; the rest stay as separate products, which makes strict accuracy slightly pessimistic.
